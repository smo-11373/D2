# Phase 6 — What D2 Does for the D1 Designer

*Phase 6 describes the functionality D2 provides, organized by **what the D1 Designer does**. For each step of his work, D2 gives him functionality that makes that step easy — and each is justified from the constitution (Phases 1–5). The test applied throughout: **write only what the D1 Designer cares about.** How anything is stored is out of scope; it matters only insofar as it lets him query and navigate his own project.*

---

## 1. Setting up the run — D2 offers default choices

The D1 Designer's first step is to set up the design run. He wants this to be easy: to get going from a good default, not to fill in a configuration document.

So D2 offers him **default design choices**. It presents a small number of high-level **postures** — for example *Standard*, *High Harness*, or *Lean* — and he picks one, or simply accepts the one D2 recommends. That single choice settles the many things a run would otherwise make him configure: how he and D2 will work together, how much harness and monitoring to expect, how deeply to investigate and evaluate, how much to report, when to stop for his review, how much resource to spend. He chooses the posture; D2 works out the detailed consequences.

The choice stays compact and human-oriented — a short selection, not a settings file. He is never forced to look deeper, but nothing that matters is hidden from him: if he wants, he can see what a posture entails, compare it against another, ask why a particular setting came out the way it did, or change any part before continuing. And because he may want to revisit how the run was set up, D2 keeps his setup as one thing he can return to, review, and revise — with his own changes plain to see.

**Why D2 works this way.** His time and attention are the scarce resource (Phase 1 §2.3), and his attention belongs on high-level design, not low-level configuration (Phase 1 §2.5). So D2 supplies the defaults and asks him only for a high-level choice — "the normal action should often be to accept the defaults" (Phase 3, Item 1) — resolving the rest itself (Phase 2, Principle 1). Making setup a single easy choice is D2 serving him exactly as the constitution requires.

## The rest of his journey *(outline — to be written in this same style once the frame is confirmed)*

2. **He gives D2 the starting point.** He hands over the existing system and says, however roughly, what he wants changed — and D2 takes that incomplete, informal input and does the work of making sense of it, so he never has to write a full specification. *(Phase 1 §4; Phase 3, Item 2.)*

3. **He wants to understand before he commits.** D2 studies the existing system and his intent, resolves what it can on its own, and hands him back a clear understanding and a proposed direction to react to — not a pile of open questions. *(Phase 3, Item 3.)*

4. **He sets the design in motion.** D2 proposes how it means to conduct the design — the shape of the work and the rules it will follow — as one thing he can accept, adjust, or push back on cheaply. *(Phase 4, Item 1.)*

5. **He oversees the work without drowning in it.** D2 carries the design forward and brings him only the high-leverage decisions, stopping for his review where it genuinely matters and proceeding on its own where it doesn't. *(Phase 2; Phase 4, Item 2.)*

6. **He inspects and intervenes whenever he wants.** He asks about the design or the process in plain terms and gets a straight answer; he can drill down, redirect, or lay down a rule — all through one place, without needing to know how D2 is organized inside. *(Phase 2, Principle 3; Phase 4, Item 3.)*

7. **He is asked only when it's worth it, and told what he needs.** When D2 needs his judgment it gathers its questions and asks once; when work is done it reports in his terms — what was decided, what changed, what's still open — not in machine records. *(Phase 2; Phase 3–4 reporting.)*

8. **He can check how well D2 served him.** After the design, D2 can show him what the process cost him — his time, his attention, where it went — and where D2 could serve him better next time. *(Phase 3, Item 5.)*
