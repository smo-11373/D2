*The Phase 1–5 files in this folder are **faithful, word-for-word transcriptions** of the source PDFs and
are **not edited**. But Phase 5 §Item 2 permits **Designer-originated completion / clarification /

## C-2026-07-19-1 — The D2 Assistant is a Human-Position-First position

**Completes:** Phase 5 §Item 3 (Human Position First). **Reconciles with:** Phase 2 §Principle 3
(unified interaction point) and Phase 4 §Item 3 (the Designer's single point of contact); glossary
`d2-assistant`.

**Completion.** The **D2 Assistant** is a first-class **Human-Position-First position** — the D1
Designer's single, unified point of contact with the entire D2 system. Per Item 3 it is designed as a
*position* first (a conceptual responsibility boundary with defined inputs, information, authority, and a
result it must produce) and **occupied by a D2 agent**; it is **non-human but treated position-first**,
exactly as Item 3 prescribes ("an agent may later occupy a position"). It takes its place among the
named positions alongside the D1 Designer, Design Node Builder, D1 Programmer, D1 / D0 Technical
Managers, and D0 Operator.

Its **responsibility** is to **conduct the design on the Designer's behalf and answer his queries**,
**interpret and route his input** to the right D2 function while **preserving interaction context**, and
**present his output** — completion reports, Review Stops, Clarification Requests, human-readable
summaries, with drill-down on request. D2 owns this interaction burden so the Designer never addresses an
internal node, governing authority, or service directly.

**Why (low hurdle — reconciliation, not revision):**

1. **Reconciliation.** Phase 4 §Item 3 and Phase 2 §Principle 3 already establish the unified
   interaction point, and the glossary already calls it a "position." Item 3's *enumeration* simply had
   not listed it. This completion removes that internal inconsistency; it does not overturn any
   established intent.
2. **Work conservation.** The interaction front performs **real work** — routing, presenting, answering.
   If it is not a position, that work **orphans**: no other named position owns "route the Designer's
   input" or "present his unified output." *A position that cannot be folded without orphaning its work
   is a real position.*
3. **The D2 surface as the Assistant's demands.** Treating it position-first makes the **D2 surface
   derivable** (Human Position First → Role → Action → Capability): to present monitoring reports it
   *demands* D2 expose monitoring data + report generation; to route input it *demands* a dispatch
   surface; to preserve context it *demands* session state. The Assistant is **D2's internal customer** —
   its needs define what D2 must provide — completing the chain **Designer-need → Assistant-action →
   D2-capability** that was broken while the position was missing.

**Consequences:**

- The D2 Assistant is now a **named position**, so **conformance** requires any derived role table to
  include it — closing the orphaned-work gap by construction, and resolving the Role–Action catalog's
  earlier "role-or-capability, flagged" uncertainty in favour of **role** (its capability-level surface is
  the *demand* it places on the Functionality layer, not an alternative to being a role).
- **Downstream (deliberate, separate step):** when the benchmark's constitution snapshot
  (`workspace/test/action-aggregate/environment/constitution/`) is re-synced to reflect this completion,
  blind runs will name and populate the D2 Assistant rather than orphan its work.

**Provenance.** Designer-originated completion — **D2 Designer (R-00), 2026-07-19**. Low hurdle
(Phase 5 §Item 2); consistent with established design (Phase 2 §Principle 3, Phase 4 §Item 3, glossary
`d2-assistant`). Recorded as completion, not bottom-up revision.
